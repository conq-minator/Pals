# PayPal Pay Later, currency conversion and your balance

A summary for PayPal users, written for PALS from PayPal's public help. Availability, terms and fees depend on your country; check PayPal Help for yours.

## Pay Later and Pay in 4

In some countries, PayPal lets you split a purchase into instalments at checkout. **Pay in 4** (in the US) splits an eligible purchase into four payments: the first is paid at checkout and the rest every two weeks, without interest. Longer monthly plans may also be offered, and those can charge interest.

To use it, choose PayPal at checkout and pick the Pay Later option if it's shown. PayPal decides at checkout whether you're eligible, and you see the payment schedule before you confirm. Missed payments can have consequences described in the plan's terms, so read them before you agree.

## Paying in another currency

When you pay a seller in a different currency, PayPal can convert the money for you. PayPal's conversion rate includes a currency conversion spread, which is why the amount can differ slightly from rates you see elsewhere. At some checkouts you can choose to let your card issuer convert instead; the checkout page shows the options.

PayPal can't process payments in every currency. Some, such as Indian rupees for cross-border payments, aren't supported in every case, so the seller or app may quote a different currency.

## Your PayPal balance

- Money people send you, and refunds, land in your PayPal balance (or go back to the card or bank you paid with, for refunds).
- You can use your balance to pay, or **transfer it to your linked bank account** from your wallet. Standard transfers are usually free and take a few business days; instant transfers, where offered, may have a fee.

## Why a payment is pending or on hold

- **Pending**: a payment can show as pending while it's being processed, for example a payment funded by a bank account, or one PayPal is reviewing.
- **On hold**: if you sell items, PayPal may hold money you receive for a period, for example for new sellers or until the buyer confirms delivery. The transaction details say why and when the money is expected to be released.

## Paying without an account

Many stores let you pay with a debit or credit card through PayPal's checkout without creating a PayPal account. Purchase protection and some features need a PayPal account.
