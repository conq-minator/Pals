# PayPal basics for shoppers

A short summary of how everyday PayPal features work, written for PALS from PayPal's public help. For exact fees, limits and the full rules in your country, check PayPal Help (paypal.com/cshelp) and the PayPal User Agreement.

## How to pay with PayPal at an online store

1. At the store's checkout, choose **PayPal** (or the yellow PayPal button) as the payment method.
2. A PayPal window opens. Log in to your PayPal account. If you don't have one, many stores also let you pay by card through PayPal without an account.
3. Choose how to pay: your PayPal balance, a linked bank account, or a linked debit or credit card. You can change the default before you confirm.
4. Review the amount and the shipping address, then confirm (the button usually says **Pay Now**, **Complete Purchase** or **Continue**).
5. You return to the store, which confirms the order. PayPal sends you a receipt by email, and the payment shows in your PayPal activity.

The store never sees your full card or bank details; PayPal shares only what the store needs to complete the order.

## How to send money to someone

1. In the PayPal app or on paypal.com, choose **Send**.
2. Enter the person's name, email address or mobile number.
3. Enter the amount and currency.
4. Choose the type of payment when asked: paying **friends and family**, or paying for **goods and services**. Only payments for goods and services can be covered by PayPal's purchase protection, so use that option when you buy something from a person.
5. Review and send.

## Linking a card or bank account

In your PayPal Wallet, choose **Link a card** or **Link a bank** and follow the steps. PayPal may ask you to confirm a bank account or card, for example with a code or small test amounts, before you can use it.

## Getting a refund

Refunds come from the seller. Ask the seller first; when they refund you, the money goes back to how you paid (PayPal balance, bank or card). Refunds to a card can take several business days to show on your card statement.

If the seller won't help, you can open a dispute in PayPal's Resolution Center.

## When something goes wrong with a purchase

- **The item never arrived**, or **it's significantly different from the description**: open a dispute in the Resolution Center. You have 180 days from the payment date. A dispute starts as a conversation with the seller and can be escalated to a claim, which PayPal decides.
- **A payment you didn't make** (unauthorised): report it to PayPal straight away and change your password.

## Keeping your account safe

- PayPal will never ask for your password, full card number or security codes by email, text or phone.
- Check that you're on paypal.com before you log in, and turn on two-step verification in your security settings.
- Forward suspicious emails or texts that claim to be from PayPal to PayPal, then delete them.

## Fees

Paying for purchases with PayPal is generally free for buyers. Fees can apply to some transfers, for example sending money funded by a card, sending money abroad, or converting currencies. Fees change and differ by country, so see PayPal's fee page for your country for current amounts.
