# PayPal buyer protection, disputes and claims

A summary for shoppers, written for PALS from PayPal's public help and PayPal's dispute documentation. The exact rules, and which purchases are eligible, are in the PayPal User Agreement for your country.

## What buyer protection covers

PayPal's purchase protection can refund you for an eligible purchase paid with PayPal when:

- **the item never arrived** (PayPal calls this "item not received"), or
- **the item is significantly different from how the seller described it** (PayPal calls this "significantly not as described"), for example the wrong item, a damaged item, or missing parts.

It applies to payments for goods and services. Money sent to friends and family is not covered, and some kinds of purchases are excluded; the User Agreement lists them.

## How to open a dispute

1. Log in to PayPal and go to the **Resolution Center** (on the web, under Help, or from the transaction in your activity).
2. Choose **Report a problem**, pick the transaction, and say what went wrong.
3. Describe the problem and add any evidence, such as photos, messages with the seller, or tracking details.

You have **180 days from the payment date** to open a dispute.

## What happens next

1. **Inquiry (up to 20 days).** You and the seller message each other through the Resolution Center to sort it out. The seller can refund you, offer a partial refund, or explain. Many disputes end here.
2. **Claim.** If you can't agree within the 20 days, either of you can **escalate the dispute to a claim**. PayPal then asks both sides for evidence and decides, usually **within 10 days** of the escalation.
3. **Decision and appeal.** If PayPal decides in your favour, you get the refund. Either side can appeal a decision they think is wrong.

Some problems go straight to a claim without the inquiry stage: billing errors, duplicate charges, and payments you didn't authorise.

## Dispute or chargeback?

A **dispute** is opened with PayPal, in the Resolution Center. A **chargeback** is when you ask your card issuer or bank to reverse a card payment instead. If you paid with a card through PayPal, you can choose either, but not both for the same problem; starting with a PayPal dispute is usually faster.

## Payments you didn't make

If you see a payment you didn't authorise, report it in the Resolution Center straight away, change your PayPal password, and turn on two-step verification. Unauthorised payments are handled as claims.

## Tips

- Contact the seller first; most problems are solved without a dispute.
- Keep receipts, photos and messages until the dispute is closed.
- Answer PayPal's requests for information before the deadline shown in the Resolution Center, or the case may close.
